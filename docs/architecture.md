# How it works

A tour of the main modules. The file table in [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) lists every module; the conventions shared by Rust and the shaders are there too.

- **`src/camera.rs`**: an orbit camera. Its orientation is the view center plus a bearing, so every orientation including the exact poles is representable. It handles picking (screen point → lat/lon), projection (lat/lon → screen point), and trackball panning that makes the surface follow your cursor; in north-up mode the roll is then snapped back to north (or to south-up after tilting over a pole). The shaders only see its `rotation()` matrix, so they work at any bearing. When you zoom, the point under the cursor stays where it is.
- **`src/geo.rs`**: fetches the compact binary layers (converted from [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/) GeoJSON) and rasterizes the land mask with Canvas 2D into an equirectangular texture. The rasterizing runs off the main thread in `assets/raster-worker.js`, a small hand-written classic Web Worker that decodes the same binary format and draws with `OffscreenCanvas`, then hands back the pixels run-length coded (`src/runs.rs`: the 8192-wide mask is a few MB instead of 32), which the main thread decodes and uploads a 256-row strip at a time, so the raw mask never sits in memory whole (the page stays responsive during load). Without `OffscreenCanvas` or workers it draws on the main thread instead, with identical output. The texture is 8192×4096 on capable desktops and 4096×2048 on touch, low-memory (`navigator.deviceMemory` of 4 GB or less) and 4096-limited GPUs. Append `?tex=low` or `?tex=high` to the URL to force a tier. The texture is a single `R8` channel holding land with lakes cut out. Coastlines and borders are not baked in: they stay vector data.
- **`src/country_cycle.rs`**: which countries are visible and in what order, and the Tab/`[`/`]` stepping rules (browser-free, unit tested).
- **`src/country.rs`**: decodes the country polygons and attribute table (also lazy-loaded), hit-tests a lat/lon against them (bounding-box prefilter, then even-odd point-in-polygon over outer rings and holes) and, for pointer input, `hit_near`, which adds a screen-space pixel tolerance for tiny islands, and rasterizes a country ID per texel (`index + 1`, 4096×2048, one byte each) with a scanline fill, in slices that yield to the event loop (about 45 ms in total). `src/info_panel.rs` fills the panel in `index.html`.
- **`src/lines.rs`**: browser-free geometry for coastlines (land and lake outlines) and country borders. Shapes become unit-sphere segments, so the ±180° seam and the poles need no special case. Antimeridian and pole edges that Natural Earth adds when cutting polygons are dropped, then each line is simplified with Douglas–Peucker at four tolerances. The renderer picks the coarsest level whose error stays under about half a pixel at the current altitude and screen size (about 77k coast segments at full detail, 4.9k at the coarsest).
- **`src/projection.rs`**: the flat projections' forward and inverse math (equirectangular, Mercator, Robinson from its 5° table with linear interpolation, Mollweide by Newton iteration), the `Projection` enum, the morph timeline and how many copies of the world the line pass draws. It is browser-free and unit tested (round-trips, the seam, poles, Mercator clamping, NaN and infinity). The GLSL in `globe.frag` (inverse), `morph.vert` and `line.vert` (forward) cannot run natively, so tests parse their constants and Robinson tables out of the shader source and compare them with the Rust values, require the functions shared by the two vertex shaders to be textually identical, and run a Rust port of the shader's Mollweide iteration in `f32`.
- **`src/render.rs`** and **`src/shaders/`**: WebGL2 draws one full-screen triangle. The fragment shader ray-traces the sphere for each pixel, converts the hit point to lat/lon, and samples the texture. Close up, it thresholds the mask so land edges stay crisp. Before that, a line pass draws the segments as instanced quads, 1 to 1.25 CSS px wide at every zoom and hidden past the horizon, into a screen-sized `RG8` target (coast, borders) that the globe shader reads, so lines get the same lighting, night tint and monochrome colours as the fill. It also adds the graticule, lighting and atmosphere. A settled flat map uses the same fragment shader with `u_mode = 1`: each pixel is inverted through the projection to a latitude and longitude, so picking, shading and the line pass agree exactly. While morphing (`u_mode = 2`) a 1° lat/lon mesh is drawn instead, with `morph.vert` placing each vertex at a blend of its globe position (points behind the limb are pinned to it, so they peel off as the globe unrolls) and its flat position; the line pass blends endpoints the same way. On a flat map `line.vert` converts each segment's endpoints to latitude and longitude, draws one copy per visible world (clipped to the outline for Robinson and Mollweide), and hides Mercator segments beyond ±85°. The morph and flat draws are full-screen, and the globe quad optimization applies only to the globe. The glacier mask is another `R8` texture, mipmapped, at half the map's width (4096×2048, 11 MB, or 2048×1024, 3 MB on the small tier): the worker rasterizes the glacier and ice-shelf polygons into it alongside the land mask, `Layers` keeps the worker's run-length coded pixels so a context restore re-uploads them without refetching, and `globe.frag` samples it with the map's UV and gradients, thresholds it at 0.5 when magnified and cuts it to the land. Monochrome stays grey and Satellite shows its own ice. The country-ID raster is a second `R8` texture (4096 wide, 8 MB, or 2048 wide, 2 MB on the small tier); the shader tints the texels whose ID equals `u_highlight`. The same raster colors the Political layer: each country has a color index (0 to 5) computed by `convert_geojson.py` and stored in the country file, and `globe.frag` maps it through a six-color palette via `u_idColor`. The converter finds neighbors as countries that share a boundary edge, then colors them with DSatur, so no two neighbors share a color (a native test checks this on the ID raster at 1024, 2048 and 4096 wide). Natural Earth's `MAPCOLOR7` is not used because it needs seven colors. Edges stay smooth by blending: bilinear weights when a texel is under a pixel, and a cubic B-spline blur over 4×4 texels, strongest country wins, when magnified (the raster is about 10 km per texel, so its edges can sit a few km off the vector borders). So close in, `id_detail.rs` rasterizes the same IDs (today's countries, or the historical year's polities) again for a lon/lat window around the view, at about one CSS pixel per texel (at most 2048×2048, 4 MB), once a world texel spans 3 px or more; `globe.frag` reads that window (`u_idsDetail`, `u_idWin`) wherever it covers the pixel, so the fills keep matching the borders at every zoom. The window covers the view and half as much again on each side, and is made again when the view leaves it, zooming makes its texels too coarse or too fine, or the world raster changes (a new year); while the view moves that happens at most every 150 ms. Where no window covers the view center, the fills still fade as before: `render::political_fill_amount` measures how many CSS pixels one ID texel covers at the screen center (through `Camera::pick`, so it holds in every projection) and passes a 0 to 1 strength as `u_political`, full up to 6 px per texel and gone at 12 px; at 0 the shader skips the fill lookups. Deep time never fades: its land mask and IDs come from the same raster. Texels with no country are ignored so fills run to the coast. Ice, drawn only inside Natural Earth's glaciated areas and Antarctic ice shelves (see [data](data.md)), overrides fills at 80%, so Antarctica and Greenland's ice cap read as ice while Siberia and Alaska do not. Until the country data arrives the layer shows plain land, then switches to the fills; Monochrome is unchanged. Hover only changes that uniform, so it redraws once per country change, not per mouse move.
- **`src/sun.rs`**: low-precision solar position (declination, plus equation of time via right ascension minus sidereal time) giving the subsolar point and the world-space sun direction for the `u_sun` uniform. Accurate to about 0.01°.
- **`src/scrub.rs`** and **`src/scrub_ui.rs`**: the time scrubber. `scrub.rs` is browser-free and tested: UTC civil date maths, the slider values to and from Unix time (minute of day, day of year, leap years, year ends), key steps, formatting of the date and local time, and the `Player` that steps time for play (continuous, or coarse for reduced motion). `scrub_ui.rs` reads and writes the panel's controls.
- **`src/geodesic.rs`**: WGS84 inverse geodesic (distance between two latitude/longitude points), pure and unit tested against GeographicLib reference values. Used by `src/measure.rs` for the reported leg and total lengths.
- **`src/view.rs`**: formats the HUD text, picks the scale bar length, defines the `Units` cycle (km, mi, nmi), parses the URL hash, and defines the `Layer` enum. A layer is a shader palette selected by the `u_layer` uniform; a layer that needs data declares it with `Layer::data_url`, and `layer_loader` fetches it on first selection. The Satellite layer (`u_layer = 2`) is the first: `main.rs` downloads `data/blue_marble.webp`, `src/imagery.rs` decodes it with `createImageBitmap` (which also downsizes it to 2048x1024 on the small texture tier), and `Renderer::upload_imagery` stores it as an `RGBA8` texture with mipmaps on texture unit 3: 44.7 MB at 4096 wide (high tier) or 11.2 MB at 2048 wide (low tier), allocated only once the layer is first opened. The encoded file (about 0.7 MB) stays in memory and is decoded and uploaded again after a lost WebGL context, so recovery makes no request; the decoded bitmap is closed after upload. `globe.frag` samples the image with the same `textureGrad`, polar LOD scale and longitude-seam handling as the land mask, in the globe, flat and morph paths. Coastlines and borders are drawn lighter over it, the night side is darkened (no city lights), the country highlight is a light wash, and the Political fills are off. The Terrain layer (`terrain.webp`) and the Night lights layer (`night_lights.webp`) are image layers drawn the same way (also `u_layer = 2`); Night lights also sets `u_imageEmissive`, which skips the key light, the ocean glint and the day/night tint, because the image is already night. All three share the one texture: `App::image_slot` records which picture it holds, `upload_imagery` writes a same-size image into the existing texture (so a switch never holds both in GPU memory), and the replaced layer is evicted from `layer_loader`, so selecting it again decodes its cached bytes before it is shown, with no new download.
- **`src/credits.rs`** and **`src/credits_ui.rs`**: the data credits panel. `credits.rs` is browser-free and tested: a static table of datasets (name, license, links, credit line) and `visible_credits(layer)`, which gives Natural Earth on every layer and adds NASA Blue Marble on Satellite and NASA Black Marble on Night lights. A test reads the "In use" table of `DATA_LICENSES.md` and fails if a dataset there has no entry, so the two cannot drift. A new dataset that needs credit goes in both. `credits_ui.rs` fills the `#credits` dialog (rebuilt when the layer changes) and moves focus in on open and back to the "Data credits" button on close; `Esc` closes it, and keys typed inside it are not map shortcuts.
- **`src/thematic.rs`** and **`src/legend_ui.rs`**: the thematic (choropleth) layers, `pop`, `gdp` and `income`, which color each country by a statistic. They need no download: the country file carries `gdp_md`, an income rank and the two estimate years (country format version 3). `thematic.rs` is browser-free and tested: it turns a country's attributes into a class (fixed breaks, so the legend can name exact ranges: population below 1M, then 10M, 30M, 100M, 300M and 1B; GDP per capita below $1k, then $2k, $5k, $10k, $20k and $40k; the five income groups), treats a missing, zero, negative or non-finite value as "no data", and builds the legend text, the palette and the "mostly 2019" source note. Classes are per country ID and reuse the Political path: `Renderer::set_theme_classes` fills the same `u_idColor` table with classes instead of palette indexes, and `globe.frag` looks them up in `u_theme` (the palette, uploaded from `Theme::palette`, so legend swatches and map agree), blending, highlighting and fading out at close zoom exactly like the Political fills. The palette is sampled from viridis (sequential, colorblind-safe, light enough to read on the dark ocean); "no data" is grey. The legend is an HTML list in `#legend` (not canvas), shown only on a thematic layer: bottom right beside the controls on desktop, above the HUD in two columns on phones, where the country info panel moves up to clear it. Because three more buttons would not fit at phone width, the thematic layers share one **Data** `<select>` in the layer picker.
- **`src/layer_loader.rs`**: browser-free state machine for lazy layer data (idle, loading, ready, failed). While a layer loads, the globe keeps the previous layer and the picker shows a busy state; the latest selection wins, stale results never switch the view, a failure falls back with a visible message, and selecting the layer again retries. `main.rs` only runs the fetches it asks for.
- **`src/search.rs`** and **`src/search_ui.rs`**: offline place search. `search.rs` is browser-free: it parses `data/places.tsv`, folds case and accents (`zurich` finds `Zürich`), and ranks exact, then prefix, then word-prefix, then substring matches, then typo-tolerant matches (bounded Damerau-Levenshtein on names and aliases, also against name and word prefixes: 1 typo allowed for queries of 4-6 characters, 2 for 7 or more, none below 4, so `zurihc`, `pairs` and `sao paolo` work), with population as the tiebreak. `highlight_segments` finds the part of a result name that matched (on the folded text, mapped back to the original characters, so `zur` marks `Zür`); typo-tolerant and alias matches show no highlight. `search_ui.rs` wires the search box and results list (matches are drawn in `<mark>` elements built from text, never `innerHTML`), and fetches `places.tsv` the first time the box gets focus, not at startup.
- **`src/labels.rs`** and **`src/label_layer.rs`**: place labels for countries, major cities, oceans and seas. `labels.rs` is browser-free. Each frame the view changes it picks candidates (a per-kind, per-tier altitude gate so density grows with zoom; countries, oceans and seas must also be big enough on screen to hold their name), drops anything on or past the horizon (labels fade out near the limb), orders them by priority (oceans, countries, then cities by population tier, with labels already visible winning ties so they don't flicker), and places them greedily, skipping any whose box overlaps one already placed (at most 90). Anchors go through `Camera::project`. `label_layer.rs` measures text once, fades labels in and out (instantly when `prefers-reduced-motion` is set) and paints them on the `#labels` canvas, which has `pointer-events: none` so hover, click and drag reach the globe. It does nothing on idle frames. Labels sit under the HUD, search box and panels, so those cover them.
- **`src/controls.rs`**: a browser-free input state machine. It takes plain events (pointer down/move/up, wheel, tick, zoom, fly-to, reset, key nudges, rotate, north-up) and mutates a `Camera`: drag, pinch, two-finger twist, alt-drag rotation, fling inertia, zoom anchoring and flights (which also turn the bearing). It is unit tested natively.
- **`src/main.rs`**: wires up the DOM, translates input events (pointer, wheel, keyboard) into `controls::Event`s and runs the animation loop, which only redraws when something changes.
