# Data

Where every dataset comes from, how it is converted, and how the files are served. Licenses and required credits are tracked in [`DATA_LICENSES.md`](../DATA_LICENSES.md); the conventions for adding data are in [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md).

## Base map, search and labels

Map data is from [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/) (1:50m land, lakes, glaciated areas, Antarctic ice shelves, admin-0 boundary lines and admin-0 countries, whose names, ISO codes, continent, subregion and population estimate feed the info panel, plus the thematic layers' statistics, which are Natural Earth's own estimates stored in the countries file: population `POP_EST` (`POP_YEAR`, mostly 2019), GDP `GDP_MD` in million US dollars (`GDP_YEAR`, mostly 2019, divided by the population for GDP per capita) and the income group `INCOME_GRP`; they are rough, and a few small territories have none, and 1:10m populated places for search and labels, and 1:50m marine polygons for ocean and sea names). It is in the public domain.

The GeoJSON sources live in `data-src/`. The app downloads compact binary files (`assets/data/*.bin`, quantized to 1e-4° and delta/varint encoded, about 680 KB instead of 6.4 MB; the country file is about 294 KB, fetched after the first frame). After changing a source file, regenerate them with `python3 scripts/convert_geojson.py`; `scripts/check-data.sh` (CI job "Data") checks byte-for-byte that the committed files are what the script generates.

Place search uses `assets/data/places.tsv` (about 350 KB, about 135 KB gzipped), generated by the same script from the 1:50m countries and 1:10m populated places in `data-src/`. It holds name, country, coordinates, population (for ranking), the center and size to frame for countries, and alternate names. A country is framed by all of its polygons that chain together within 15 degrees of the mainland (the polygon with the largest bounding box), so archipelagos such as Indonesia, the Philippines and Kiribati are framed whole while far-flung overseas parts (French Guiana, Hawaii) are left out. The longitude span is the smallest arc of the circle that covers them, so Fiji, Kiribati and Russia get a narrow frame across the 180th meridian. `scripts/test_frame_box.py` tests this and runs in `scripts/check-data.sh`. It is downloaded only when the search box is first focused.

Labels use `assets/data/labels.tsv` (about 125 KB, about 55 KB gzipped): country label points, populated places with at least 100,000 people plus all national capitals, and oceans and seas, each with a tier that sets how far in it appears. It is generated by `convert_geojson.py` and fetched right after the first frame. Ocean and sea label points come from Natural Earth's `ne_50m_geography_marine_polys`: `scripts/marine_label_points.py` picks one point inside each polygon's largest ring and writes `data-src/ne_50m_marine_label_points.geojson`. Re-run it only when updating that source.

## How data files are served and checked

The app never fetches these files by name. After every build (including `trunk serve`), a Trunk hook (`scripts/hash_data.py`) moves each file to `data/files/<name>.<hash>.<ext>` and writes `data/manifest.json`, which maps the original names to the hashed paths. The app loads the manifest once (`src/data.rs`; `index.html` preloads it so it costs no extra round trip) and resolves every data file through it. Hashed files never change, so `assets/_headers` caches them as immutable, and only the small manifest revalidates (every 60 s). Paths in the manifest are relative to it or absolute URLs, so the data can later be served from another host without changing the app.

After a build, `python3 scripts/validate_data.py dist/data` checks the manifest, content hashes, size limits and that every file decodes with coordinates in range (CI runs it). Given `--baseline DIR` (the published data), it also refuses a layer that vanished or lost more than half of its bytes or features, unless it is named in `--allow-shrink`.

Licenses and required credits for every dataset, including the ones planned for the roadmap, are tracked in [`DATA_LICENSES.md`](../DATA_LICENSES.md).

## Image layers

The Satellite layer uses `assets/data/blue_marble.webp` (4096x2048 lossy WebP, about 0.7 MB), downloaded only the first time the layer is selected. It is NASA Earth Observatory's [Blue Marble: Next Generation w/ Topography and Bathymetry](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/blue-marble-next-generation/base-topography-bathymetry), July 2004 (Reto Stockli and Jesse Allen; ocean depth shading from GEBCO), resampled from the 5400x2700 JPEG at <https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/esd/eo/images/bmng/bmng-topography-bathymetry/july/world.topo.bathy.200407.3x5400x2700.jpg>. NASA says its imagery is generally not subject to copyright in the United States, asks that NASA be acknowledged as the source (see its [media usage guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/)), and asks for the credit "NASA Earth Observatory" on Blue Marble images. NASA insignia and logos are not covered, and none is used. The source's URL, size and sha256 are pinned in `data-sources.lock.json`. `python3 scripts/make_blue_marble.py` fetches it through `scripts/data_sources.py` (cached in `.data-cache/`, an error if the hash differs) and rebuilds the file (needs Pillow with WebP; the original is not committed). To move to a new upstream file, run `python3 scripts/data_sources.py bump nasa-blue-marble-200407 --url ... --version ...` and commit the lockfile. CI cannot download from NASA and the encoder output varies between versions, so the "Data" job does not regenerate this file: it checks that it exists, is a 4096x2048 WebP and is under 2 MB.

The Terrain layer uses `assets/data/terrain.webp` (4096x2048 lossy WebP, about 0.9 MB), downloaded only the first time the layer is selected. It combines two public-domain Natural Earth 1:50m rasters, [Cross Blended Hypso with Shaded Relief and Water](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/50m-raster-data/50m-cross-blend-hypso/) (`HYP_50M_SR_W`) and [Ocean Bottom](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/50m-raster-data/50m-ocean-bottom/) (`OB_50M`), version 2.0.0: the sea floor's relief, divided by that raster's flat land color, is multiplied into the hypsometric image, so land is unchanged and the oceans gain shelves, ridges and trenches. Both downloads (about 150 MB together) are pinned in `data-sources.lock.json` as `ne-50m-hypso-relief-water` and `ne-50m-ocean-bottom`. `python3 scripts/make_terrain.py` fetches them through `scripts/data_sources.py` and rebuilds the file (needs Pillow with WebP, and NumPy). Like the satellite image, CI does not regenerate it: `scripts/check-data.sh` checks that it exists, is a 4096x2048 WebP and is under 2 MB.

The Night lights layer uses `assets/data/night_lights.webp` (4096x2048 lossy WebP, about 0.3 MB), downloaded only the first time the layer is selected. It is NASA Earth Observatory's [Earth at Night](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/earth-at-night/maps/) (Black Marble) 2016 color composite from Suomi NPP VIIRS, resampled from the 13500x6750 JPEG; credit "NASA Earth Observatory images by Joshua Stevens, using Suomi NPP VIIRS data from Miguel Román, NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center". It falls under the same NASA media guidelines as Blue Marble. The source is pinned in `data-sources.lock.json` as `nasa-black-marble-2016`; `python3 scripts/make_night_lights.py` rebuilds the file (needs Pillow with WebP), and CI only checks its shape.

The Population density layer (Data menu, `9`, `&layer=density`) shows where people live regardless of borders: people per km² in 2020 on a 4096×2048 grid (about 10 km at the equator), colored on a log scale from 0.1 to 50,000 over the monochrome map, with a legend. It uses `assets/data/population_density.webp` (a lossless grey WebP, 0.9 MB, downloaded on first use; it shares the image layers' texture), built by `scripts/make_population_density.py` from the JRC's [GHS-POP R2023A](https://human-settlement.emergency.copernicus.eu/ghs_pop2023.php) 30 arc-second grid (CC BY 4.0, pinned as `ghs-pop-e2020-30ss`, a 482 MB download; needs NumPy, Pillow, tifffile and imagecodecs). Each pixel holds a code for the log density, and `globe.frag` maps it through the color ramp in `view::DENSITY_RAMP` (sent as `u_densityRamp`), so the colors can change without rebuilding the data.

## Overlays

The languages are `assets/data/languages.tsv` (0.7 MB, downloaded when the layer opens), converted by `scripts/convert_glottolog.py` from [Glottolog](https://glottolog.org) 5.4 (Hammarström, Forkel, Haspelmath and Bank; glottolog-cldf, CC BY 4.0): the 8,081 languages with a location (not dialects, and not the "Bookkeeping" entries), with their family, macroarea and endangerment status; and from [Grambank](https://grambank.clld.org) 1.0.3 (Skirgård et al. 2023, *Science Advances*; CC BY 4.0): 15 features for the about 2,400 languages it codes, joined by glottocode.

The societies are `assets/data/societies.tsv` (80 KB), converted by `scripts/convert_dplace.py` from [D-PLACE](https://d-place.org) 3.3 (Kirby et al. 2016; dplace-cldf), which carries the Ethnographic Atlas (Murdock et al.): its 1,291 societies, their locations and focal years, and six coded variables (EA042, EA043, EA012, EA030, EA033, EA066). D-PLACE is CC BY-NC 4.0: using it depends on Biruni staying non-commercial (see `DATA_LICENSES.md`).

The historical borders are `assets/data/history_borders.bin` (3.3 MB, downloaded the first time the view opens), converted by `scripts/convert_cliopatria.py` from [Cliopatria](https://github.com/Seshat-Global-History-Databank/cliopatria) v0.2.1 (Seshat Global History Databank; Bennett et al. 2025, *Scientific Data*), CC BY 4.0: about 13,400 polity records from 3400 BCE to 2024, each valid for a span of years, simplified to 0.08° and recolored so neighbors differ. The format (HIST) is described in [`historical-data.md`](historical-data.md).

Their language families are `assets/data/history_languages.tsv` (68 KB, downloaded when Lang is first pressed), converted by `scripts/convert_seshat_languages.py`. First from the [Seshat Global History Databank](https://seshat-db.com)'s Polaris 2026 release (Turchin et al.): each polity's `polity_language` and `polity_linguistic_family`, joined to Cliopatria's records by their Seshat IDs, with family names tidied to top-level families. Where Seshat has nothing, from [Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org) (CC0): the official languages (P37) of the Wikidata item Cliopatria names for the polity (or its languages used, P2936, when it has no official one and at most three), each language's family taken from Glottolog by the language's Glottolog code. The Wikidata answers are a committed snapshot, `data-src/wikidata_polity_languages.tsv`, fetched by `scripts/fetch_wikidata_languages.py` (about 10 requests to the Wikidata Query Service; rerun it by hand to refresh, then rerun the converter). Seshat's data is CC BY-NC-SA, so the combined file is offered under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and kept apart from the borders (see [`DATA_LICENSES.md`](../DATA_LICENSES.md)).

Deep time uses `assets/data/paleo_coastlines.bin` (110 KB: the model's present-day coastline polygons with their plate and age range, simplified to 0.1°) and `paleo_rotations.tsv` (190 KB: its 5,943 finite rotations), converted by `scripts/convert_merdith.py` from the Merdith et al. (2021) plate model v1.2.4 (EarthByte, *Earth-Science Reviews* 214; CC BY 4.0). The browser composes the rotations (interpolating between listed ages) to reconstruct any age; tests check reconstructed points against the GPlates Web Service.

## Sky view

The sky view's stars are `assets/data/stars.bin` (about 420 KB, downloaded the first time the sky is opened): the 41,486 stars to magnitude 8 of the [HYG database](https://codeberg.org/astronexus/hyg) v4.4 by David Nash (astronexus), with positions, magnitudes, B-V colors and 474 proper names. HYG is CC BY-SA 4.0, so `stars.bin` is a derived work offered under CC BY-SA 4.0 as well, and it is kept in its own file. The source is pinned as `hyg-v44`; `python3 scripts/convert_stars.py` rebuilds the file (stdlib only, deterministic), and CI checks that it decodes. The view shows stars down to about magnitude 6 at a 100° field and fainter as you zoom in (to 8 at about 10°); star size and brightness follow the magnitude, color follows B-V, and the brightest named stars in view get labels.

The constellation figures are `assets/data/constellations.bin` (about 10 KB, fetched with the stars): the 88 constellations of [Stellarium's sky cultures](https://github.com/Stellarium/stellarium-skycultures) `western` culture (text and data CC BY-SA), each a set of polylines between Hipparcos stars whose positions come from HYG, with its Latin name at the mean direction of its stars. It is a derived work offered under CC BY-SA 4.0, kept in its own file. The figure file is pinned as `stellarium-western`; `python3 scripts/convert_constellations.py` rebuilds it, and CI checks that it decodes.

The Milky Way behind the stars is `assets/data/milky_way.webp` (2048x1024 lossy WebP, about 0.3 MB, fetched with the stars): the star-free Milky Way map of NASA's [Deep Star Maps 2020](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4851) in celestial coordinates, made from Gaia DR2; credit "NASA/Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization Studio. Gaia DR2: ESA/Gaia/DPAC". The source (a 4096x2048 OpenEXR file) is pinned as `nasa-svs-milkyway-2020-4k`; `python3 scripts/make_milky_way.py` tone maps and resamples it (needs OpenEXR, NumPy and Pillow), and CI only checks its shape. The sky shader adds it faintly at night and not at all by day.

The deep-sky survey is the [Digitized Sky Survey](https://archive.stsci.edu/dss/) in color as HiPS tiles (Hierarchical Progressive Surveys, on the HEALPix grid), made by CDS (CNRS/Unistra) and fetched at view time from the ESA ESASky and NASA/IPAC IRSA mirrors (CDS's own copy answered 404 at the time). Nothing of it is stored or served by Biruni, except a reduced copy of its Allsky atlas used as a visual-test fixture. DSS images are licensed CC BY-NC-SA, for scientific, educational and non-profit use; credit "Digitized Sky Survey - STScI/NASA, Colored & Healpixed by CDS", with plate copyrights held by Caltech (POSS-II), UK SERC/PPARC and the Anglo-Australian Observatory (UK Schmidt), and AURA. Using it depends on Biruni staying non-commercial (see `DATA_LICENSES.md`).

## Solar system view

The solar system view's surface maps (`assets/data/planet_*.webp`, 2048×1024 each, 0.1 to 0.6 MB) are built by `scripts/make_planets.py` from US government works: the Moon from NASA/GSFC SVS's CGI Moon Kit (LRO LROC color), Mars from USGS Astrogeology's Viking MDIM 2.1 color mosaic (fetched from its Wikimedia Commons copy), Mercury from MESSENGER's global mosaic (PIA15063, contrast-stretched), Jupiter from Cassini's map (PIA07782), and the Earth from the Blue Marble. Sources are pinned in `data-sources.lock.json`.
